三个「啊哈」时刻 · Three Aha Moments

本次旅程中真正改变我认知的三个判断
1

医生的第一步是查病因，不是上治疗
Test the cause first, not the treatment

诊断环节是循证的：先做真菌检查，拿到阳性结果后再定方案。但治疗方案（刷酸）只处理「已经长出的痘」，无法回应「为什么长痘」——真正的诱因是压力、睡眠与饮食，而这些不在医疗系统的处方范围之内。

Diagnosis was evidence-based; the treatment addressed only the visible acne, not why it grew — stress, sleep and diet lie beyond what medicine can prescribe.

2

支付结构在诊室里悄悄分流
Payment structure splits quietly inside the clinic

同一次就诊中，挂号与检查由医保覆盖，刷酸全部自费。一条医保目录线把一次就医切成两半：一半行为由医保规则塑造，另一半由患者的自费意愿决定。

One insurance line split the visit in half: consultation and tests covered, peels out of pocket — half shaped by insurance rules, half by my own willingness to pay.

3

疗效黑箱，最后由「心理安慰」补位
The efficacy black box, filled by psychological comfort

没有对照组、没有随访指标、没有结果记录，患者无法回答「值不值」。我至今无法区分是刷酸起了作用，还是工作节奏改变起了作用，抑或本会自愈。

No control group, no follow-up metrics, no outcome records — the patient cannot answer "was it worth it": the peel, the lifestyle change, or natural recovery?

English Version · 英文版

Dermatology visit, Beijing Chaoyang Hospital (associate chief physician)

I visited the dermatology department of Chaoyang Hospital, Beijing, for recurrent acne on my face, scalp and back. The doctor, an associate chief physician, ordered a fungal test before proposing any treatment; the result came back positive. The recommended treatment was a chemical peel (刷酸), three sessions, prescribed as a package.

The payment split is worth noting: the consultation and the lab test were covered by public insurance, whereas the peels were not — all out of pocket. I completed all three sessions.

The twist came later: after I moved to a less stressful job and study rhythm, the acne largely stopped. Was it the peel, or the lifestyle change? I honestly cannot tell. During the treatments, it at least felt like something was being done — psychological comfort substituting for unmeasured outcomes.

Three observations follow. 1) Diagnosis was evidence-based, yet the intervention treated symptoms rather than causes — the real cause, namely stress, sleep and diet, lies outside what medicine can prescribe. 2) One insurance line split a single visit into covered and uncovered halves. 3) With no outcome measurement at all, the patient substitutes perceived experience for efficacy — precisely the gap that Porter's value-based agenda targets.

My own words at the time: "To be honest, I still do not know whether the peel actually worked against the acne, but after each session I felt a little more at ease." (translated)

坐骨神经痛开药

Sciatica & the Prescription Mix · 2022, Shanghai lockdown · chief complaint: sciatic pain from all-day desk work with zero exercise

经过 · What Happened

2022 年上海封城期间，居家办公、久坐且缺乏运动，出现坐骨神经痛。就诊后医生开具西药，服药后症状明显缓解；同一处方中另含一组中成药。我的直观判断是：真正起效的是西药，中成药更接近「搭着开」。

During the 2022 Shanghai lockdown, I developed sciatica from all-day desk work without any exercise. The doctor prescribed Western medicines, which clearly worked, plus a stack of Chinese patent medicines. My layman's read is that the Western drugs did the work, while the TCM part functioned as an add-on.

English Version · 英文版

Sciatica & the Prescription Mix · sources retrieved November 2026

During the 2022 Shanghai lockdown, I developed sciatica from all-day desk work without any exercise. The doctor prescribed Western medicines, which clearly worked, plus a stack of Chinese patent medicines. My layman's read is that the Western drugs did the work, while the TCM part functioned as an add-on.

What do the clinical references say? According to the sources retrieved in November 2026 — the Tencent Medical Dictionary entry on sciatica, and public answers by an orthopedic associate chief physician at China-Japan Friendship Hospital — first-line therapy consists of 1) NSAIDs (ibuprofen, celecoxib, diclofenac sodium) to relieve pain and reduce nerve-root edema; 2) neurotrophic agents (mecobalamin, active vitamin B12) to promote nerve repair; and 3) muscle relaxants (eperisone) as adjuncts, while Chinese patent medicines (e.g., Yaotongning and Yaobitong capsules) are listed as conventional co-prescriptions, "used at the physician's discretion based on syndrome differentiation."

To conclude, the "NSAIDs ± mecobalamin" combination is the most common outpatient pairing in China, which matches my personal experience that the Western drugs worked; the TCM co-prescription is likewise part of the listed routine, not an isolated incident (my inference). If "effective Western drugs plus add-on TCM" is a widespread prescribing habit, two explanations are possible: 1) volume-based incentives under fee-for-service payment (Herzlinger's six forces — Players × Funding); or 2) clinical tradition compounded by patient expectations. One data point cannot settle the question, and that is precisely the discussion I want to bring to class.
